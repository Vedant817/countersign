# Adversarial review — why this loses, and what to do about it

Two critics were spawned on 24 October 2026 with one brief: **argue why this project fails and wins
nothing.** One played a hackathon judge scoring the five Devpost criteria; one played a payments
domain expert. Neither was asked to be fair, and neither was.

Their findings are recorded here in full because **the value is in the objections, not the fixes** —
a list of things we already intended to do is worthless, whereas a list of things we cannot answer is
the actual risk. My own assessment follows each group.

## Group A — the thesis is aimed at the wrong dispute (fatal if unaddressed)

The strongest objection, and the one I think is correct.

**A1. Intent is not the disputed fact in the disputes that cost merchants money.** SNAD ("not as
described") and INR ("item not received") turn on *delivery and item condition*. A signed mandate is
silent about both. The one case where intent genuinely decides — `UNAUTHORISED` — is adjudicated by
the issuer or PayPal, not by the merchant. So "provable intent" addresses the minority of claims and
cannot move the majority.

**A2. The liability router picks an action; it does not assign liability.** Card chargebacks are
decided by the buyer's issuer under network rules; PayPal's in-house claims are decided by PayPal.
`packages/defender/src/router.ts` mapping `UNAUTHORISED → USER_AUTHORISED → CONTEST` is a local
verdict about a decision someone else makes. The four labels are not states any PayPal API returns.

**A3. SCA/MIT.** Buyer-absent stored-credential variable-amount is precisely the case where
authorisation risk stays with the merchant. A signed intent is not an SCA method and shifts no
liability. In the EU/UK/India the merchant's unauthorised-payment exposure is unchanged by anything
in this repository.

**A4. The evidence packet has no slot for its own innovation.** `provide-evidence` takes a fixed
`evidence_type` enum plus capped notes and documents (`packages/paypal/src/types.ts:710-720`). The
signed chain, SD-JWT, decision record and QR-to-verifier ride as an unstructured blob — and for a card
chargeback the decider is the issuer, which never adjudicates a mandate.

**A5. The evidence is generated by the accused party.** The checkout JWT is signed by Countersign's
single `VI_MERCHANT_KID`, shipments are merchant-asserted via `/track`, and merchant-fault rules
require the merchant to file evidence against itself. EC-MER-4 already concedes we cannot detect
faked delivery.

**My read:** A1–A3 are correct and they are the project's real problem. The honest repositioning is
available and narrower: **Countersign does not decide liability, it produces a tamper-evident record
that a decider can use, and it refuses to assert what it cannot prove.** That is defensible. "We route
liability deterministically" is not, and the pitch says the latter.

The fix is mostly *positioning plus one real feature*: state plainly that SNAD/INR are out of scope
because we do not know delivery truth, and make the `UNAUTHORISED` case excellent — because there, a
signed on-device approval genuinely is evidence no merchant currently has.

## Group B — trust and strategy (serious)

**B1. Trust collapses to "trust Countersign".** The agent key is held server-side by vi-service; the
merchant's Gate and the public verifier both call Countersign's own `/v1/verify`. There is no
independent anchor a merchant or PayPal can check, so "verifiable by anyone" is a website vouching for
itself. Partially mitigated by the public verifier accepting keys from JWKS, but not answered.

**B2. PayPal already holds this.** PayPal has the transaction record, Seller Protection and dispute
tooling, and runs its own agentic-commerce programme. A third party adding a parallel verdict layer
onto PayPal's rail is a risk-ops and support burden, not a product. The mandate format is
Mastercard/Google's Verifiable Intent — a competitor's rail.

**B3. No merchant integrates a new verification layer to accept a payment they already accept.** The
Gate is a per-checkout dependency, dispute filing needs API access to the merchant's PayPal account,
and the benefit is an unproven win-rate.

**My read:** B1 is fair and cheap to improve (publish the verification recipe so a sceptic can check a
record without our server — `verify.py` already exists for this). B2 is not answerable by code and
should be *acknowledged* rather than argued with; the honest line is "this is what we would build
inside PayPal", which is also a stronger pitch to a PayPal judge than pretending to compete with them.

## Group C — real defects worth fixing now

**C1. Griefing is built into the Gate.** Check 12 suspends a mandate after **3 consecutive BLOCKs**.
Any listing or catalogue can knock a user's agent offline by repeatedly presenting one out-of-mandate
cart. **This is a genuine security defect**, not a product objection, and it is fixable.

**C2. The threat model confuses key *export* with key *use*.** Non-extractability stops exfiltration;
it does not stop script on the wallet origin driving passkey-backed signing. The design then contests
the user it just failed to protect. §13 concedes this but the pitch does not.

**C3. `/status` claimed this instance can "move money on PayPal".** Fixed in `a4341c3`.

## Group D — presentation (fixable, currently zero)

**D1. `/judge` is a list of what the product does not do.** The first deliberate judge experience is
an apology.

**D2. The purchase dead-ends at a quote.** "Lock price" lands on a page whose own banner says no order
was placed. Criterion 2 (a coherent product) is failed at the terminal action.

**D3. The lab is titled "Lab · AT-M5" and prints base64 signatures.** A judge sees a test harness.

**D4. There is no AI in the product.** `packages/agent/src/index.ts` is `export {}`; nothing in
`apps/**` imports `ai` or `@ai-sdk/openai`. The event requires AI. **This is the single largest gap.**

**D5. The repo is private and nothing is deployed.** Deferred by Vedant, both required at submission.

## What I would do, in order

1. **Wire the model in (D4)** — the Modal endpoint is deployed and `OPENAI_BASE_URL` works. One real
   LLM call site in the product beats any amount of documentation about one.
2. **Fix the griefing defect (C1)** — a suspension triggered by an attacker-controlled input.
3. **Build the merchant surface (D2, D1)** — AG Studio, per `PRIZE-STRATEGY.md`. It converts a
   dead-end into a product and repairs criteria 2 and 3.
4. **Reposition the pitch (A1–A3)** — narrow the claim to what is provable, and say why SNAD/INR are
   out of scope. A judge who spots the overclaim unaided stops believing everything else.
5. **Fix `/judge` (D1)** — stop opening with what is missing.

## The uncomfortable summary

The engineering in this repository is real: 2,688 tests, a deterministic Gate with twelve checks, a
public verifier, honest refusals where a value cannot be determined. None of the five judging criteria
reward that on its own.

What is missing is a **product a judge can use in ninety seconds**, and an **AI component**. Those are
the two things standing between this and any prize — not correctness, and not effort.
